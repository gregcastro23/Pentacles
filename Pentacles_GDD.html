00 — Overview

A persistent war for the visible heavens.

Pentacles is a real-time, location-based AR MMO. Players are sorted into ten planetary factions drawn from their real birth chart, granted a Tarot deck procedurally seeded by the exact degrees and minutes of their natal planets, and sent to battle for control of the night sky — which the game overlays with a single, sky-spanning Pentacle divided into eleven capturable zones.

Genre

Location-based AR · Persistent MMO · Collectible-deck tactics · Territory control

The Hook

The map is not invented — it is the literal sky above you, and the front line moves as the planets do, in real time.

Session Shape

5–15 min skirmishes outdoors at night; async siege & deck-tuning anywhere, any time of day.

01 — Design Pillars

Four commitments that govern every decision.

I

The Sky Is Sacred

The map is real and shared. Two players in the same city see the same zones, the same owners, the same stars — only the angle differs.

II

Identity Is Earned

Your faction and deck come from you — a real chart, not a class menu. No two starting decks are mechanically identical.

III

Time Is A Mechanic

Real ephemeris drives ownership pressure. Your planet transiting a zone is a tangible, exploitable advantage.

IV

Always Playable

Cloud, daylight, indoors — a simulated sky falls back onto the ceiling so the game never asks for a clear night to function.

02 — Onboarding

Character creation is the natal chart.

Chart & faction scoringP1Top-3 choiceP1Swiss-grade precisionP4

There is no class select. The player supplies three facts; the game computes a real astrological chart and uses its geometry to assign a faction and forge a deck.

Input

A handle (display name) plus three facts: birth date, birth location (geocoded to lat/long), and birth time. If time is unknown, it defaults to 12:00 local noon and the player is flagged solar-chart (Houses are suppressed, see below).

Processing

A Swiss-Ephemeris-grade calculation resolves the positions of all ten bodies to the arc-minute, plus the Ascendant and Midheaven. This runs client-side once at creation and the resolved chart is committed to the server as immutable player data.

Faction Assignment

The engine scores the chart against each planet using a weighted dignity vector: chart ruler (Ascendant lord) ×3, Sun & Moon sign rulers ×2, stellium & angular planets ×1.5, plus essential dignity (rulership / exaltation) bonuses.

The top three scoring planets are surfaced as a choice — the player commits to one, preserving agency while keeping the result grounded in their real chart.

Output

An immutable NatalChart record, a chosen Faction, and a deterministic deck seed derived from the chart's exact placements.

Implementation — as built

The shipped chart math is game-grade, not yet Swiss-Ephemeris: unity/ChartCalculator.cs and feeder/ephemeris.ts use JPL low-precision Keplerian elements with a truncated Sun/Moon series — sub-degree for the Sun and planets, ~1° for the Moon. Ample for sign & zone placement; a Swiss-Ephemeris swap is the production target.

The live scorer (chart::faction_scores) weights chart-ruler ×3, Sun & Moon sign-rulers ×2, angular bodies +1.5, a per-body dignity term, and a stellium bonus (+1.5 for any body in a sign holding 3+ placements). The three-way choice and its server-side re-validation (create_player rejects any faction outside the chart's top-3) ship as described.

Design intent: the three-way choice converts a deterministic algorithm into a meaningful identity moment, and sidesteps the “I don't want to be a Saturn” churn risk of pure assignment — while keeping every option astrologically true to the player.

Decks that grow — Sky Drops ShippedP2

The starting deck is your chart; the deck you end with is your conquest. Every star you capture mints one new card — a Sky Drop forged from the star's brightness and the planet transiting its zone. Because the reward is drawn from the sky you just took rather than a shop, a grown deck still reads as yours: Pillar II holds even as the collection widens.

Trigger

Guaranteed — one card per successful star capture, whether the win came from an Auto-Siege resolve or a live Lane Skirmish duel. No roll to miss.

Suit & source

The planet currently transiting the captured star's zone sets the drop's suit (its biased_suit) and source_body — you literally collect the body that ruled the sky overhead. If no planet transits that zone, it falls back to the capturer's own faction bias.

Power tier

The star's apparent magnitude → rank/tier via the same node_weight that scores its control: 1st-magnitude objective stars (Vega, Sirius) drop high-rank cards; faint stars drop pips. Brighter sky, better spoils.

Sub-stats

Fine stats roll from a battle seed (star id + tick + victor) computed inside the authoritative reducer, so drops are deterministic and server-minted — never client-supplied, never RNG the client can fish.

Power cap

Drop stats are clamped below your hero trump and at most on par with a top natal pip. Winning widens your options, not your raw ceiling — progression is breadth, not power creep.

Collection cap

A hard cap (default 60, set in GameConfig). At the cap, a new drop replaces your single weakest card only if it is stronger; otherwise it is logged and discarded — guaranteed rewards stay meaningful without a dusting economy or forced inbox to clear.

Landing

New cards arrive on the Bench (Loadout::Bench), exactly as mint_deck overflow does. Your Active 8 and Sentinel never change on their own — you promote a drop when you want it.

05 — The Celestial Pentacle

The sky, divided into eleven contested zones.

11-zone geometryP0Star catalogueP0

A single Pentacle is projected across the celestial hemisphere from horizon to horizon. A pentagram inscribed in an outer circle partitions the visible sky into eleven capturable territories. Every real, visible star falls into exactly one zone and becomes a node within it.

5 × Spires
The star's points — small triangles. Few stars, high strategic value as chokepoints between houses.
1 × Crown

Implementation — as built

The eleven zones are seeded on first publish (init → 5 House, 5 Spire, 1 Crown) and the horizon-anchored geometry lives in unity/PentacleGrid.cs. Star nodes are populated today by a demo set of 8 bright stars (Sirius, Canopus, Arcturus, Vega, Capella, Rigel, Procyon, Polaris) spread across the zones; the full Hipparcos / Yale Bright Star catalogue load is planned. Each star caches its current zone in region_hint, refreshed server-side. The eleven-zone adjacency graph is now encoded in the crate (zone_neighbors) and backs the Crown's dominion and Jupiter's snowball (§07).

Implementation — as built

Round-based attrition & veterancy ShippedP2

Auto-Siege resolves as a round-based attrition battle (combat::simulate_battle): each round both sides deal suit-scaled damage at once, the weakest cards that damage can afford are destroyed, and the survivors heal to full — repeating until one side is wiped (or a round cap of 8, then the stronger remnant takes it). Durability is health + armour; healing is per-round, so only destruction persists within a fight.

Cards that survive earn XP and level up at thresholds — small permanent bumps to attack, health and armour, capped at level 10. Veterans harden; the fallen on both sides feed Pluto's per-zone attrition (§03, §07).

Victory in a battle

Wipe the opposing side — destroy every enemy card before yours fall — and bank control equal to your surviving board strength. Survivors carry their XP into the next siege.

07 — Zone Control & Win Conditions

Tug-of-war over zones, fought at the stars.

Star→zone controlP2DecayP3Always-on warP3Transit buffP3Seasons & CrownP3Pluto attritionP3AlliancesP3

A zone is too large to “win” outright. Instead each zone holds a control meter (−1000 … +1000) and a scatter of star-nodes. You don't capture the zone directly — you capture its stars, and the stars move the meter.

1 · Battle a star

Each visible star is a capturable node. Tap it, fight the duel (live or async). Brighter stars (lower magnitude) are weighted nodes worth more control.

2 · Shift the meter

A won star adds its weight to your faction's tally in that zone and subtracts from the current holder. The meter is the sum of all node allegiances.

3 · Flip & hold

Cross the threshold and the zone flips to your faction. Held zones decay over time, so territory must be actively defended, not parked.

Star weighting

Node value scales with apparent magnitude — a 1st-magnitude star (e.g. Vega, Sirius) is worth many times a faint one, creating natural “objective” stars players rally around.

Sky-drop reward

Every star you capture also mints a new card, forged from that star's brightness and the planet transiting its zone — the deck grows from the sky it conquers. Full rules in §04 · Sky Drops.

Shipped — minted by resolve_star_battle and the duel resolver.

Decay

Uncontested control bleeds toward neutral at a fixed rate, modified by faction passives (Saturn slow, Moon nightly slow). This prevents permanent lockout and keeps the map fluid.

The Great Wheel

A season is one full revolution of the 360° zodiac. An Ingress Marker advances a degree at a time across the wheel; each 30° crossing (a sign ingress) fires a mid-season event that re-weights a region of the sky. Completing all 360° resolves the season.

Transit bonus

When a faction's real planet is currently transiting a zone, every capture there is amplified — the live sky is a rotating buff that no one controls.

Always-on war

The map never sleeps: any faction with no human players sends bots to raid a star in the zone its planet is transiting, so all ten factions keep contesting territory even on an empty server.

Shipped as bot_raid — up to two raids per sky tick, each nudging that zone's meter.

Win conditions

Zone: hold the control meter past threshold. Crown: the central pentagon is the keystone — its owner buffs all adjacent contests. Season: when the Marker completes the 360° wheel, the faction holding the most weighted zones is crowned and the map soft-resets — winners keep a head-start edge, never a locked board.

Implementation — as built

Meter: Zone.control ships as a single-sided 0..1000 meter for whoever currently owns the zone. An attacker's wins subtract from the owner; at the zero-crossing the zone flips and the attacker takes it with the overflow (capped at FLIP_THRESHOLD = 600). The symmetric −1000…+1000 above is the design framing. Star weighting (node_weight, brighter = worth more) and the win-margin bonus (control_delta) ship as described.

Decay: tick_sky bleeds every held zone toward 0 each 10 s — Saturn 4, Moon 5, all others 8 — releasing it at 0. Transit buff: a capture under your own transiting planet is amplified ×1.5 (capture_multiplier); the Crown buffs its adjacent contests ×1.25 for its owner and Jupiter snowballs +15% per adjacent zone it holds, over a static zone_neighbors graph. The Great Wheel now turns: tick_sky advances season_degree one degree per tick, logs each 30° sign ingress, and at 360° resolves the season with a soft-reset (holds halved, owners kept). Pluto attrition: every card destroyed in a zone's battles drops a stack (attrition table) that lifts Pluto's captures there (+3% per kill, capped +60%) and expires after ~30 ticks. Venus alliance: a Venus capture spills 25% of the control it banks into each adjacent allied-held zone (venus_spillover), and a zone flanking Venus territory defends at ×1.25 — over the fixed combat::relation graph that also blocks allies from besieging each other and grants rivals +15% attack. All multipliers are tunable consts.

08 — AR & Real-Time Ephemeris

Anchoring a fixed grid to a turning sky.

Sky rendererP0Ephemeris feedP0Indoor fallbackP4

The hardest technical question: the stars move, the planets move, the player turns — but the Pentacle must feel like a stable, shared structure. The answer is a two-frame system.

The Pentacle is fixed to the horizon, not the stars

The grid is anchored in the local horizontal (alt-azimuth) frame: outer circle = horizon, Crown = zenith, one spire locked to true North. It does not rotate with the Earth. This means the same physical patch of sky over your city is always the same zone — essential for a shared, capturable map.

The stars & planets move through it

Stars and planets are placed in the equatorial frame (RA/Dec) and projected into the horizontal frame per the player's lat/long and current time. So nodes drift across zone boundaries through the night — a star can change which zone it belongs to, and planets transit zones in real time, exactly as the design demands.

Pose pipeline

SourceProvides
GPSObserver lat / long / altitude
Device clockUTC → local sidereal time
MagnetometerTrue-north heading (azimuth)

The client computes a horizontal-frame ray from the device pose, intersects it with the Pentacle to know which zone is centred, and ray-hits star-nodes for selection. Heavy ephemeris is precomputed; the device only interpolates.

Why Unity is the render target

For a celestial AR renderer with thousands of star points, real planetary bodies, and a fallback indoor projection, Unity 6 with AR Foundation is the recommended stack — with SpacetimeDB reached over its Rust/C# SDK.

Unity + AR Foundation

One codebase over ARKit & ARCore. Mature plane detection for the indoor ceiling-projection fallback; GPU instancing for the full star catalogue.

SpacetimeDB C# SDK

Subscribe to map & battle tables; call reducers directly from Unity. State syncs as a live local cache — ideal for an authoritative MMO map.

Why not WebXR

Sensor fusion, sky-scale rendering, and AR plane mapping are too heavy and inconsistent on mobile WebXR today. Keep web for a read-only spectator map.

Why not Unreal

Unreal renders beautifully but its mobile-AR + lightweight-MMO tooling is thinner; Unity's AR Foundation + asset ecosystem ships this faster.

Render-the-stars note: load a real star catalogue (Hipparcos / Yale Bright Star, ~9k naked-eye stars) as a single instanced point cloud; planets are individual billboards placed from the ephemeris. Both live in the equatorial frame and are transformed into Unity world-space each frame by the horizontal-frame matrix — this is the piece you prototype first in Unity.

Implementation — as built

The two-frame transform ships in unity/SkyMath.cs and the P0 renderer SkyRenderer.cs, with PentacleGrid.cs for the horizon-anchored grid. Ephemeris is produced off-module by the Bun feeder (feeder/ephemeris.ts, game-grade JPL Keplerian) and pushed to the ephemeris table via the owner-gated push_ephemeris reducer — the in-module tick_sky does not compute astronomy. The catalogue is currently the 8 demo stars; the full Hipparcos point-cloud and the indoor ceiling-projection fallback are still ahead.

09 — SpacetimeDB Backend

Authoritative state in Rust.

Module liveP1–P3

SpacetimeDB holds the entire game as a single, live, transactional database. Tables are the state; reducers are the only way to mutate it. The map, every player, every deck, and every in-flight battle live here — clients subscribe and react.

Now shipped as real code

The full module lives in /server — a buildable Rust crate: types · tables · chart · combat · reducers. The snippets below are excerpts; the crate is the source of truth.

Nearly every table is public and streams to clients as the live map — player, card, deck_slot, zone, star_node, ephemeris, game_config, duel & duel_queue; only natal_chart stays private to its owner. A scheduled sky_tick_timer drives tick_sky every 10 s. Clients never write state — they call reducers, which validate and mutate transactionally, so the map is cheat-resistant by construction.

Implementation — as built

The crate builds against the modern SpacetimeDB Rust API and ships init, create_player, resolve_star_battle, enqueue_duel, commit_duel, tick_sky and push_ephemeris. One deviation from the original sketch: tick_sky does not compute astronomy — RA/Dec and transiting_zone arrive from the off-module feeder via push_ephemeris; the tick decays zones, runs bot_raid, advances season_degree, and purges expired attrition stacks.

10 — Core Loop

One session, end to end.

01

Look Up

Raise the device; AR resolves your pose and renders the Pentacle over the real sky (or your ceiling).

02

Read the Map

See zone owners, your faction's front line, and where your planet is transiting right now.

03

Pick a Star

Tap a contested node — a bright objective star or a quiet flank.

04

Duel

Fight live or against the owner's Sentinel deck; suits & chart stats decide it.

05

Shift & Hold

Move the zone meter, bank control and a fresh sky-drop card, set your own defense — then chase the next star.

Implementation — as built

The shipped slice of the loop: Look Up renders via SkyRenderer (P0); Pick a Star → Duel runs through resolve_star_battle (Auto-Siege) or enqueue_duel/commit_duel (live Lane Skirmish); Shift & Hold moves Zone.control and reads back from the Defense (Sentinel) loadout. Captures under your transiting planet are amplified (§07), and a sky-drop card (§04) is banked on every win.

Spread Duel — the third combat model

Cards laid into a Tarot spread; past / present / future positions grant real bonuses. Turn-based, async-first, deeply on-theme (§06).

Hook: a third branch on the resolver + a spread/position schema, selected by CombatModel::SpreadDuel.

CombatModel season switch

The enum exists and rides on BattleLog, but nothing dispatches on it. Make it a season-level A/B switch with no client rebuild (§06).

Hook: an active_model field on GameConfig + dispatch in the combat reducers; clients read it.

Aspect → synergy slots

Trine / sextile aspects between natal bodies become combo links between the cards they minted (§04, §06). Computed once at mint time.

Hook: a synergy field on card (or an aspect side-table) + a combo term in resolve_star / lane_power.

Also queued: the full Hipparcos / Yale catalogue load (P0), a fuller 40-card deck plus richer per-card abilities & a draw-pile loop (P2), Swiss-Ephemeris precision (P4), the indoor ceiling-projection fallback & spectator web map (P4).
