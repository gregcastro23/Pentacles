00 — Overview

A persistent war for the visible heavens.

Pentacles is a real-time, location-based AR MMO. Players are sorted into ten planetary factions drawn from their real birth chart, granted a Tarot deck procedurally seeded by the exact degrees and minutes of their natal planets, and sent to battle for control of the night sky — which the game overlays with a single, sky-spanning Pentacle divided into eleven capturable zones.

Genre

Location-based AR · Persistent MMO · Collectible-deck tactics · Territory control

The Hook

The map is not invented — it is the literal sky above you, and the front line moves as the planets do, in real time.

Session Shape

5–15 min skirmishes outdoors at night; async siege & deck-tuning anywhere, any time of day.

North Star

“Look up, point your phone, and fight for the constellation you were born under.”

01 — Design Pillars

Four commitments that govern every decision.

I

The Sky Is Sacred

The map is real and shared. Two players in the same city see the same zones, the same owners, the same stars — only the angle differs.

II

Identity Is Earned

Your faction and deck come from you — a real chart, not a class menu. No two starting decks are mechanically identical.

III

Time Is A Mechanic

Real ephemeris drives ownership pressure. Your planet transiting a zone is a tangible, exploitable advantage.

IV

Always Playable

Cloud, daylight, indoors — a simulated sky falls back onto the ceiling so the game never asks for a clear night to function.

02 — Onboarding

Character creation is the natal chart.

There is no class select. The player supplies three facts; the game computes a real astrological chart and uses its geometry to assign a faction and forge a deck.

Input

Birth date, birth location (geocoded to lat/long), and birth time. If time is unknown, it defaults to 12:00 local noon and the player is flagged solar-chart (Houses are suppressed, see below).

Processing

A Swiss-Ephemeris-grade calculation resolves the positions of all ten bodies to the arc-minute, plus the Ascendant and Midheaven. This runs client-side once at creation and the resolved chart is committed to the server as immutable player data.

Faction Assignment

The engine scores the chart against each planet using a weighted dignity vector: chart ruler (Ascendant lord) ×3, Sun & Moon sign rulers ×2, stellium & angular planets ×1.5, plus essential dignity (rulership / exaltation) bonuses.

The top three scoring planets are surfaced as a choice — the player commits to one, preserving agency while keeping the result grounded in their real chart.

Output

An immutable NatalChart record, a chosen Faction, and a deterministic deck seed derived from the chart's exact placements.

Design intent: the three-way choice converts a deterministic algorithm into a meaningful identity moment, and sidesteps the “I don't want to be a Saturn” churn risk of pure assignment — while keeping every option astrologically true to the player.

05 — The Celestial Pentacle

The sky, divided into eleven contested zones.

A single Pentacle is projected across the celestial hemisphere from horizon to horizon. A pentagram inscribed in an outer circle partitions the visible sky into eleven capturable territories. Every real, visible star falls into exactly one zone and becomes a node within it.

Fig. 1 — The eleven zones: 5 arc-houses, 5 spires, 1 crown

5 × Arc-Houses
Large three-sided zones, one curved edge resting on the horizon circle. The bulk territory and most stars.
5 × Spires
The star's points — small triangles. Few stars, high strategic value as chokepoints between houses.
1 × Crown
The central pentagon at the zenith. The keystone zone — owning it buffs every adjacent contest.

Whichever model is active, suits form a directed advantage cycle with one cross-cutting support axis. An attack into a suit it beats lands at ×1.5; into a suit that beats it, ×0.66.

07 — Zone Control & Win Conditions

Tug-of-war over zones, fought at the stars.

A zone is too large to “win” outright. Instead each zone holds a control meter (−1000 … +1000) and a scatter of star-nodes. You don't capture the zone directly — you capture its stars, and the stars move the meter.

1 · Battle a star

Each visible star is a capturable node. Tap it, fight the duel (live or async). Brighter stars (lower magnitude) are weighted nodes worth more control.

2 · Shift the meter

A won star adds its weight to your faction's tally in that zone and subtracts from the current holder. The meter is the sum of all node allegiances.

3 · Flip & hold

Cross the threshold and the zone flips to your faction. Held zones decay over time, so territory must be actively defended, not parked.

Star weighting

Node value scales with apparent magnitude — a 1st-magnitude star (e.g. Vega, Sirius) is worth many times a faint one, creating natural “objective” stars players rally around.

Decay

Uncontested control bleeds toward neutral at a fixed rate, modified by faction passives (Saturn slow, Moon nightly slow). This prevents permanent lockout and keeps the map fluid.

The Great Wheel

A season is one full revolution of the 360° zodiac. An Ingress Marker advances a degree at a time across the wheel; each 30° crossing (a sign ingress) fires a mid-season event that re-weights a region of the sky. Completing all 360° resolves the season.

Transit bonus

When a faction's real planet is currently transiting a zone, every capture there is amplified — the live sky is a rotating buff that no one controls.

Win conditions

Zone: hold the control meter past threshold. Crown: the central pentagon is the keystone — its owner buffs all adjacent contests. Season: when the Marker completes the 360° wheel, the faction holding the most weighted zones is crowned and the map soft-resets — winners keep a head-start edge, never a locked board.

08 — AR & Real-Time Ephemeris

Anchoring a fixed grid to a turning sky.

The hardest technical question: the stars move, the planets move, the player turns — but the Pentacle must feel like a stable, shared structure. The answer is a two-frame system.

The Pentacle is fixed to the horizon, not the stars

The grid is anchored in the local horizontal (alt-azimuth) frame: outer circle = horizon, Crown = zenith, one spire locked to true North. It does not rotate with the Earth. This means the same physical patch of sky over your city is always the same zone — essential for a shared, capturable map.

The stars & planets move through it

Stars and planets are placed in the equatorial frame (RA/Dec) and projected into the horizontal frame per the player's lat/long and current time. So nodes drift across zone boundaries through the night — a star can change which zone it belongs to, and planets transit zones in real time, exactly as the design demands.

Pose pipeline

SourceProvides
GPSObserver lat / long / altitude
Device clockUTC → local sidereal time
MagnetometerTrue-north heading (azimuth)
Gyro + accelDevice tilt / where it's aimed
AR trackingDrift-corrected 6-DoF stabilisation

The client computes a horizontal-frame ray from the device pose, intersects it with the Pentacle to know which zone is centred, and ray-hits star-nodes for selection. Heavy ephemeris is precomputed; the device only interpolates.

Why Unity is the render target

For a celestial AR renderer with thousands of star points, real planetary bodies, and a fallback indoor projection, Unity 6 with AR Foundation is the recommended stack — with SpacetimeDB reached over its Rust/C# SDK.

Unity + AR Foundation

One codebase over ARKit & ARCore. Mature plane detection for the indoor ceiling-projection fallback; GPU instancing for the full star catalogue.

SpacetimeDB C# SDK

Subscribe to map & battle tables; call reducers directly from Unity. State syncs as a live local cache — ideal for an authoritative MMO map.

Why not WebXR

Sensor fusion, sky-scale rendering, and AR plane mapping are too heavy and inconsistent on mobile WebXR today. Keep web for a read-only spectator map.

Why not Unreal

Unreal renders beautifully but its mobile-AR + lightweight-MMO tooling is thinner; Unity's AR Foundation + asset ecosystem ships this faster.

Render-the-stars note: load a real star catalogue (Hipparcos / Yale Bright Star, ~9k naked-eye stars) as a single instanced point cloud; planets are individual billboards placed from the ephemeris. Both live in the equatorial frame and are transformed into Unity world-space each frame by the horizontal-frame matrix — this is the piece you prototype first in Unity.

09 — SpacetimeDB Backend

Authoritative state in Rust.

SpacetimeDB holds the entire game as a single, live, transactional database. Tables are the state; reducers are the only way to mutate it. The map, every player, every deck, and every in-flight battle live here — clients subscribe and react.

Now shipped as real code

The full module lives in /server — a buildable Rust crate: types · tables · chart · combat · reducers. The snippets below are excerpts; the crate is the source of truth.

// ── Identity & natal chart ─────────────────────────────
#[spacetimedb::table(name = player, public)]
pub struct Player {
    #[primary_key]
    identity:      Identity,          // SpacetimeDB auth id
    handle:        String,
    faction:       Planet,            // chosen faction enum
    deck_seed:     u64,               // deterministic deck key
    created_at:    Timestamp,
    last_active:   Timestamp,
}

#[spacetimedb::table(name = natal_chart)]   // private to owner
pub struct NatalChart {
    #[primary_key]
    identity:      Identity,
    birth_unix:    i64,               // resolved birth instant (UTC)
    birth_lat:     f64,
    birth_lon:     f64,
    time_known:    bool,              // false ⇒ solar chart, noon default
    // 10 bodies, each packed as degree*60+minute within sign (0..16200)
    placements:    Vec<Placement>,
    ascendant:     u16,
    midheaven:     u16,
}

pub struct Placement {
    body:          Planet,
    sign:          u8,                // 0..11
    arc_minutes:   u16,               // 0..1799 within sign
    retrograde:    bool,
    dignity:       i8,                // −5 fall .. +5 rulership
}

pub enum Planet { Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars,
                  Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto }
